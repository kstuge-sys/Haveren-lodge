Something for every season

Lofoten is a good place to be in every season. You just need to know what to expect. Because of the northern location, the light and the weather change a lot through the year, and each season has its own character.

Winter

From around early December to early January, the sun stays below the horizon. What you get instead is a soft blue light and long dark evenings, with a fairly good chance of seeing the northern lights dance across the sky.

Winter storms are part of the season too. If you want to experience the raw force of nature, this is the time, preferably from a seat by the window.

It’s also the ski season, typically good from February to April. Snow conditions can change quickly because of the coastal climate, but there are plenty of options for touring, from the mountaintops down to the sea. A local guide can show you where the best snow is.

Winter climbing is possible for those who are keen. The cod season is also in winter, from around January to April, and it’s famous here.

Spring

Bright days, snow still on the peaks, and the light coming back fast. Good for hiking and climbing, and often still good for skiing early in the season.

Summer

The midnight sun shines from around late May to mid July. You can go hiking at midnight and it will feel like the middle of the afternoon.

Summer is the season for golf at Lofoten Links, horseback riding, and climbing. There are mountains and routes for every level. You can also get out on a boat tour to see sea eagles, and with some luck, whales.

Surfing is possible in summer for everyone. For the more experienced and robust, it’s an all-year activity.

Autumn

The surf season picks up, the dark skies return, and so does the chance of northern lights. Hiking is good as long as the weather is on your side.

All year

  • Fishing is an all-year activity.
  • Scenic drives are always possible, whatever the weather.
  • Hiking is great when the snow is gone. In winter, stick to gentle routes, and be careful if the slopes are steep.

Culture and food

The old fishing villages are worth a visit in any season. Nusfjord is one of the best preserved, and Henningsvær is a fishing village with a lively feel. If you’re in the mood for art, there’s a gallery in Henningsvær.

There are plenty of places to eat, though what’s open and what’s on the menu depends on the season. Ure is one of our favourites, where a Michelin-starred chef has found his new project.
